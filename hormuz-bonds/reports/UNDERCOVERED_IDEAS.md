### #1: ERII (Energy Recovery) — $9 | $550M mkt cap | 5 analysts

**The single most asymmetric idea in the entire research.**

Gulf states produce 50-70% of freshwater from desalination. Iran has already attacked Bahrain's desal plants (March 7-8). Every Gulf government just learned their WATER SUPPLY is a military target.

- ERII's PX Pressure Exchanger cuts desal energy consumption by 60%
- Just launched PX Q650 (March 9) with 63% higher peak flow
- Dominant player in pressure exchange devices (99% efficiency)
- Stock is at a 12-MONTH LOW while Gulf water security is front-page news
- Only 5 analysts cover it. Market has completely missed this

**Why ceasefire HELPS this trade:** Gulf states will have oil revenue AND motivation to invest massively in desalination hardening. Ceasefire = capex acceleration, not deceleration. This is the OPPOSITE of every other name on the list.

**Downside:** Current guidance was cut ($105-125M desal revenue) due to project delays. The stock is cheap for a reason. But the structural demand acceleration hasn't been priced in — it's a 12-24 month order cycle.

### #2: MEOH (Methanex) — $56 | $4.4B mkt cap | 15 analysts

**30-35% of global methanol supply is offline and the stock is up only 4%.**

- Iran = world's #2 methanol exporter. Saudi Ar-Razi (4+ Mtpa) stranded behind Hormuz
- Methanex = world's largest methanol producer. ZERO Gulf exposure. Production in Canada, US, NZ, Chile, Trinidad, Egypt
- Spot methanol breached $300/mt China, $400/mt Europe
- Raymond James: every $5/bbl oil = ~$150M Methanex EBITDA
- At ~6x fwd EV/EBITDA on normalized earnings — cheap even without Hormuz thesis

**Why undercovered:** Methanol is in the specialty chemicals ghetto. Generalist PMs and retail don't think about it. The stock's muted reaction (+4%) despite 1/3 of supply gone suggests the market is pricing this as temporary.

**Ceasefire risk:** Moderate. Methanol prices normalize if Gulf restarts. But damaged Qatar infrastructure + shipper risk premiums create multi-quarter tail.

## UNDERCOVERED SHORTS

### #1: AVOL.SW / AVTAF (Avolta) — CHF 58 | CHF 8.2B mkt cap

**Duty-free operator sitting on a MAG bomb.**

- World's largest duty-free operator. EMEA = 52% of revenue
- Dubai DXB = world's busiest international airport. $2.4B duty-free sales in 2025. Traffic COLLAPSED
- Hidden risk: Minimum Annual Guarantee (MAG) concession payments — they pay rent to airports EVEN WITH ZERO PASSENGERS
- Management reaffirmed 5-7% organic growth guidance — this is fantasy
- Swiss-listed = under-screened by US short sellers

**Ceasefire doesn't fix it:** Gulf tourism recovery takes 2-3 years (see: Sharm el-Sheikh post-2015). MAG payments are contractual. Cannot be renegotiated quickly.

### #2: KHNGY / KNIN.SW (Kuehne+Nagel) — CHF 172 | CHF 20B mkt cap

**Volume-based freight forwarder that can't benefit from higher rates.**

- World's largest sea freight forwarder. Earns commissions on VOLUME — doesn't own ships
- Own exec on CNBC: "Impossible to replace Strait of Hormuz port capacity"
- 130+ ships locked in Persian Gulf
- Guided 2026 EBIT down 17% — BEFORE full crisis scope was clear
- Fixed cost base erodes margins when volumes collapse

**Ceasefire doesn't fix it:** Supply chain rerouting is sticky. Insurance repricing persists for years. Gulf port rehabilitation takes years. Customers have already started diversifying.

**Key insight:** Tanker companies (FRO, STNG) OWN assets that benefit from longer routes. K+N is a MIDDLEMAN that loses when volume doesn't flow. Opposite exposure.
